# Research Studio — Question to Reproducible Result

## Purpose

Research Studio extends LabVerse beyond school practicals. It supports structured scientific inquiry in Physics, Chemistry and Biology, using source-backed knowledge, reviewed models, user datasets and reproducible analysis.

## Main workspace

1. Research project title and question.
2. Background/source library with citations.
3. Hypothesis and alternative explanations.
4. Experimental design and safety review.
5. Simulation or data collection/import.
6. Data quality and analysis.
7. Evidence-backed interpretation.
8. Reproducible report and next-test suggestions.

## Research question record

Each question contains:
- question text and research domain;
- rationale and source-linked background claims;
- scope, target population/material/system and boundary conditions;
- measurable variables and operational definitions;
- current evidence and known uncertainty;
- date, author and version.

## Hypothesis record

A hypothesis includes:
- testable statement;
- proposed mechanism or model;
- independent, dependent and controlled variables;
- quantitative/qualitative predictions;
- plausible alternative explanations;
- expected observations under each explanation;
- a falsification or discriminating test;
- assumptions, limitations and confidence label.

A hypothesis is not a fact. It remains CANDIDATE until suitable tests and review support or reject it.

## Experimental design

The protocol records:
- objective, method and model version;
- sample/material/specimen and inclusion criteria;
- controls, comparisons, sample size/rationale and repeats;
- instrument range, calibration and measurement units;
- independent/dependent/control variables;
- environment conditions and timing;
- planned analysis and stopping rules;
- safety/risk review and any required human supervision;
- deviations from the planned protocol.

The software must not invent a validated sample size or call a design safe merely because the workflow form is complete.

## Simulation and real data

Research projects can use:
- deterministic virtual experiments;
- a supported mathematical/domain model;
- user-recorded instrument readings;
- imported datasets from permitted sources;
- observations recorded in a supervised physical lab.

These data types must remain clearly distinguished. A simulation cannot be represented as a physical measurement.

## Evidence-linked analysis

Every important conclusion can link to:
- a raw observation or dataset;
- a transformation/calculation;
- an equation/model and its version;
- a primary/secondary source;
- a test or reviewer note.

Research summaries preserve disagreement and negative results. Do not discard null results simply because a hypothesis was expected.

## Research outputs

- project protocol;
- hypothesis and alternative hypotheses;
- raw and processed data tables;
- figures, units and statistical summaries;
- methods and model assumptions;
- evidence map and bibliography;
- interpretation, uncertainty and limitations;
- conclusion status: SUPPORTED, NOT_SUPPORTED, INCONCLUSIVE or NEEDS_REVIEW;
- candidate next experiments.

## AI and autonomy boundary

Optional AI may help search/summarise permitted literature, organise notes or propose candidate hypotheses. It may not fabricate papers, data, equations or results; override domain computations; claim novelty from a limited search; approve hazardous procedures; or label a discovery without independent validation. Researcher/teacher review remains essential.
