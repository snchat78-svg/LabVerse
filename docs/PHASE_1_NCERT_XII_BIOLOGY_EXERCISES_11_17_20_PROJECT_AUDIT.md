## 2. Exercise 11 — preparation and analysis of pedigree charts

The source requires family trait information across more than one generation and explains symbols for sex, marriage and generations. It asks learners to analyse pedigree patterns as autosomal dominant, autosomal recessive, X-linked dominant, X-linked recessive and Y-linked.

Examples named in the chapter include tongue rolling, widow's peak, blood groups, cheek dimples, red-green colour blindness, hitch-hiker's thumb and hypertrichosis of the ear/pinna. These are examples listed by the legacy source, not proof that each is a simple monogenic trait.

Candidate IDs:
- BIO-XII-PEDIGREE-SYMBOL-SET
- BIO-XII-PEDIGREE-GENERATION-NODE
- BIO-XII-PEDIGREE-RELATIONSHIP-EDGE
- BIO-XII-PEDIGREE-INHERITANCE-PATTERN-CLASSIFIER
- BIO-XII-PEDIGREE-UNCERTAINTY-ANNOTATION

**LabVerse decision:** use synthetic pedigree datasets with known assumed genotypes for the first teaching models. Do not infer genotype or inheritance mode from a small observed family tree without a validated genetic basis. Use “consistent with / inconsistent with / insufficient evidence”, not diagnostic labels.

**Privacy boundary:** do not ask students for names, family health histories, genetic test results, reproductive history or identifiable relatives' phenotypes. Synthetic data is the default.

## 3. Exercise 17 — plant ecological adaptations in xeric and hydric conditions

The source compares plant specimens from dry and aquatic/wet habitats and records morphological/anatomical modifications in an observation table. It names examples including cacti and succulents, Cycas leaves, pine needles, Acacia, Nerium, Parkinsonia, Casuarina, Salvinia, Eichhornia, Pistia, Hydrilla, Vallisneria, Utricularia, Limnophila, Typha, Phragmites, Marsilea and Rhizophora. The source lists beakers/glass jars, microscope, slides/coverslips and razor blades; it describes temporary stained transverse sections of leaves, stems and roots.

Candidate IDs:
- BIO-XII-PLANT-ADAPTATION-SPECIMEN-CATALOGUE
- BIO-XII-PLANT-XEROPHYTE-MODEL
- BIO-XII-PLANT-HYDROPHYTE-MODEL
- BIO-XII-PLANT-ADAPTATION-CROSS-SECTION
- BIO-XII-PLANT-ADAPTATION-OBSERVATION-MATRIX

Use virtual specimens or pre-prepared slide representations. No learner use of razor blades or unauthorised plant collection. Keep external morphology, internal sections and inference as separate observations.

## 4. Exercise 18 — animal adaptations in xeric and hydric conditions

The source examples include xeric animals such as rat, camel and squirrel, and hydric animals such as fish, frog and prawn. Its observation table covers body colour/contour, locomotory structures, integument/derivatives, mouth, respiratory organs and moisture acquisition/conservation.

Candidate IDs:
- BIO-XII-ANIMAL-ADAPTATION-SPECIMEN-CATALOGUE
- BIO-XII-ANIMAL-XERIC-MODEL
- BIO-XII-ANIMAL-HYDRIC-MODEL
- BIO-XII-ANIMAL-ADAPTATION-COMPARISON
- BIO-XII-ANIMAL-ADAPTATION-OBSERVATION-MATRIX

Use virtual models or approved prepared imagery; no live animal collection, handling or stress testing. Avoid universal claims across every species in a broad group.

## 5. Exercise 19 — pH of water and soil samples

The source describes three approaches:
1. indicator-dye method;
2. electrometric method using a pH meter;
3. colorimetric method.

It says indicator paper is less accurate than the electrometric method. The example includes universal pH indicator solution and broad-range indicator paper (pH 2–11).

Candidate IDs:
- ENV-SAMPLE-WATER-PH
- ENV-SAMPLE-SOIL-PH
- ENV-PH-INDICATOR-DYE-METHOD
- ENV-PH-INDICATOR-PAPER-METHOD
- ENV-PH-ELECTROMETRIC-METHOD
- ENV-PH-COLORIMETRIC-METHOD
- ENV-PH-CALIBRATION-AND-UNCERTAINTY

Record method, instrument/indicator version, calibration state, sample identity, resolution, replicates and uncertainty. Never present indicator-paper estimates as equivalent in precision to a pH-meter reading.

## 6. Exercise 20 — turbidity of water samples

The source connects turbidity with light penetration and photic-zone depth and describes two methods:
- Secchi disc: lower a black/white segmented disc vertically until it disappears and reappears; use the mean disappearance/reappearance depth as a photic-zone estimate.
- Measuring-cylinder comparison: three same-height 500 mL cylinders are filled with water samples and left undisturbed overnight.

Candidate IDs:
- ENV-WATER-TURBIDITY-SAMPLE
- ENV-WATER-SECCHI-DISC
- ENV-WATER-PHOTIC-ZONE-DEPTH
- ENV-WATER-SETTLING-CYLINDER
- ENV-WATER-TURBIDITY-COMPARISON

**Safety:** the source's Secchi method describes reaching the centre of a pond by boat. LabVerse must not turn this into an unsupervised field instruction. Use a simulated depth series or approved field dataset. Never prompt learners to enter water or use a boat.

## 7. Investigatory Project Work — source-derived Research Studio design

The source describes investigations as open-ended work that begins with a question or hypothesis. Learners plan variables, measurements, repeats and controls; collect data systematically; record error sources; analyse findings; evaluate and revise ideas; and communicate the result.

Its suggested report structure includes:
1. title;
2. objectives;
3. materials;
4. method and precautions;
5. results in a table/chart;
6. analysis and interpretation;
7. discussion;
8. conclusion;
9. references.

Suggested topics in the source include water pH, pH effect on seed germination, quantitative phytoplankton analysis, local biodiversity/species inventories, leaf mosaics, weeds/birds, local industry/environmental impact, chemical/pollutant effects on mitotic index, and genetic markers in a human population. These are source examples, not RBSE-prescribed project topics.

### Project safety and integrity
- Human genetic-marker work: no identifiable family/genetic data collection in the student app; use synthetic or properly governed anonymised datasets.
- Water/plankton projects: no unsupervised collection, formalin/FAA preservation or unknown organism handling.
- Industrial pollution projects: use official/public datasets or approved observations; no restricted-site entry or effluent handling.
- Chemical effects on mitotic index: virtual-only model; no chemical preparation or exposure workflow.
- Biodiversity inventories: non-invasive observation only; record sampling effort, location uncertainty and provenance.

### Proposed Research Studio data contract

| Record | Required fields |
|---|---|
| Research question | id, wording, scope, source/author |
| Hypothesis | statement, predicted relationship, assumptions, alternatives |
| Design | independent/dependent/control variables, sampling frame, inclusion rules, protocol version |
| Data capture | raw value, unit, timestamp, sample/site ID, method, instrument, replicate, observer/source |
| Quality | missingness, outlier flag, calibration, uncertainty, deviations, safety/ethics flags |
| Analysis | formula/statistical method, software/version, parameters, outputs |
| Interpretation | supported/contradicted/inconclusive, alternative explanations, limitations |
| Reproducibility | dataset/protocol hash and version, random seed where relevant |
| Report | title, objectives, materials, method, results, analysis, discussion, conclusion, references |

This is a design contract, not an implemented schema.
