# Biology Simulation Model

## Coverage target

Map the official practical scope for both classes before choosing assets. Potential categories include compound/light microscopes, slides, cover slips, stains/reagents, forceps, needles, droppers, petri dishes, models, preserved specimens, plant/animal tissues, flowers, seeds and ecological sampling materials. The definitive inventory comes from the current syllabus and manuals.

## Interactive experience

- Place microscope and slide on the workbench.
- Insert/align a supported slide, adjust focus and magnification.
- Microscope view changes according to prepared-slide asset and focus state.
- Select specimen/model, rotate and inspect labelled structures.
- Complete spotting/identification tasks and record diagnostic characteristics.
- Use structured diagrams, measurements or counts where the practical requires them.

## State and analysis

Each prepared slide/specimen has verified identity and class/subject mapping; labelled structures and visible diagnostic features; allowed magnifications or inspection states; observation prompts; expected identification and reasoning; source and review status; and known limitations.

Analysis explains which visible features support an identification. It must not claim a structure is visible at a magnification/resolution where it is not represented.

## Asset integrity

Use original or permissively licensed 3D models, slide imagery and diagrams with provenance. Biological images must be appropriate to the educational scope, labelled accurately and reviewed for misleading anatomy or scale.
