# Safety and Scientific Integrity

## Virtual-only boundary

LabVerse presents digital simulations. It does not control real heaters, flames, glassware, chemicals, electrical supplies, biological specimens or laboratory instruments. Hazardous demonstrations are labelled as virtual and supervised-learning representations. The app must not encourage students to reproduce hazardous work at home.

## Safe design rules

- Include only syllabus-mapped, reviewed substances and actions.
- Show hazard metadata and supervision context before a simulated procedure.
- Do not provide free-form instructions for arbitrary chemical mixing or ignition.
- Reject incompatible, unsupported or out-of-range actions with a clear reason.
- Do not simulate a dangerous outcome as harmless spectacle without warning.
- Avoid instructions implying students should smell, taste or directly touch chemical/biological samples.
- No real-world ignition or chemical dispensing hardware integration in the initial release.

## Scientific truth rules

1. Every non-trivial outcome references a verified rule or an explicitly labelled approximation.
2. Observation is not inference; show both separately.
3. Colour is not a unique chemical identification unless the practical and confirmatory tests justify it.
4. Bubbles do not identify a gas by themselves.
5. A visual flame is an educational representation, not a flame-temperature measurement or full emission spectrum.
6. Do not fabricate products, precipitate colours, gases, numerical readings or specimen details.
7. Show uncertainty, assumptions and model limitations.
8. Unsupported inputs return NOT_MODELLED or NEEDS_REVIEW.

## Review and traceability

Each chemistry reaction, physics equation/model and biology identification has a source, reviewer, version and regression tests. Corrections create a new content version so saved results can be reproduced.
