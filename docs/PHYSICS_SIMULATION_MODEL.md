# Physics Simulation Model

## Coverage target

Build the inventory and experiments from the official RBSE/NCERT catalogue, not from a guessed fixed list. Expected categories to audit include measurement instruments; mechanics apparatus; optical apparatus; electrical components; and thermal, sound or wave apparatus only where confirmed by the syllabus.

Potential measurement items: metre scale, Vernier calipers, screw gauge, balance and stopwatch. Potential mechanics: pendulum, spring, weights, pulleys and inclined plane. Potential optics: lenses, mirrors, optical bench, screen, pins and light source. Potential electricity: cells/power source, wires, key, resistance box, rheostat, ammeter, voltmeter, galvanometer and meter bridge. These are audit categories, not a claim that every item is required in every RBSE session.

## Physics state and interactions

- Instrument calibration, zero error, least count, range and resolution.
- Scale reading and uncertainty.
- Connections between compatible terminals/components.
- Changing resistance, length, mass, angle, focal distance or another experiment variable.
- Repeated observations and graph generation.
- Derived quantities with units and uncertainty propagation where appropriate.

## Live behaviour examples

- Connecting ammeter/voltmeter correctly enables circuit readings; changing a circuit variable updates the modelled reading.
- Wrong meter connection produces a diagnostic explanation.
- Vernier/screw-gauge readings respond to instrument scale and object measurement; least count and zero correction are explicit.
- Moving an optical object changes ray/image construction and supported measured distances.
- Changing pendulum length changes modelled period within a stated approximation.

## Accuracy contract

Use correct school-level equations and declared assumptions. Distinguish measured, ideal/theoretical, simulated and calculated values. Numerical results need units, significant figures, tolerances and test vectors. If a practical is not sufficiently modelled, provide a structured reading exercise or mark NOT_MODELLED; never imply a full physical simulation.
