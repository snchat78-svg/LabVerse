## 2. Class XI — Physics (PDF pages 114–119)

### Examination and record requirements
- Maximum practical marks: 30.
- Breakdown: one experiment 10; two activities (one from each section) 8; project 4; practical record 4; viva 4.
- Record: at least 12 experiments and at least 8 activities, with 4 activities from each section.
- Time conflict: the Hindi header on page 114 states 4 hours, while the English “Practical Examination Evaluation Scheme” on the same page states 3 hours. The platform must not choose one without clarification from RBSE/teacher.

### Experiments (17 listed)
1. Vernier calipers: external diameter of a small sphere/cylinder; dimensions and density of a regular object of known mass; internal diameter/depth and volume of a cylindrical object such as a beaker, glass or calorimeter.
2. Screw gauge: wire diameter, sheet thickness and volume of an irregular lamina.
3. Spherometer: radius of curvature of a spherical surface.
4. Beam balance: masses of two different objects.
5. Parallelogram law of vector addition: weight of a wooden block.
6. Simple pendulum: L–T and L–T² graphs and effective length of a seconds pendulum.
7. Limiting friction versus normal reaction and coefficient of friction for a block on a horizontal surface.
8. Downward component of gravitational force on a roller on an inclined plane; graph of force versus sin(theta).
9. Young’s modulus of wire material using Searle’s apparatus.
10. Helical spring force constant and effective mass using an oscillation method and T²–m graph.
11. Boyle’s law for air at constant temperature; P–V and P–1/V graphs.
12. Surface tension of water by capillary rise.
13. Liquid viscosity using terminal velocity of a spherical body.
14. Cooling curve of a hot body.
15. Sonometer: frequency versus wire length at constant tension; length versus tension at constant frequency.
16. Speed of sound in air at room temperature using a resonance tube.
17. Specific heat capacity of a solid and a liquid by the method of mixtures.

### Activities
**Section A (7):** make a paper scale with least count 0.2 cm or 0.5 cm; determine mass using a metre scale and moments; plot data with suitable scale and show error bars; measure limiting rolling friction; study water-jet range versus projection angle; study energy conservation of a ball on a double-inclined plane; study energy dissipation of a simple pendulum over time.

**Section B (6):** observe state change and plot cooling curve for molten wax; heating effect on a bimetallic strip; liquid level change on heating; detergent effect on water surface tension by capillary rise; factors affecting heat-loss rate of a liquid; load-related depression of a suitably clamped metre scale at its end and middle.

### Projects (11)
1. Investigate conservation of energy of a simple pendulum.
2. Determine radius of gyration of a metre scale used as a bar pendulum.
3. Study velocity change under constant force and determine acceleration.
4. Compare materials as heat insulators.
5. Compare materials as sound absorbers.
6. Compare Young’s modulus of rubber samples and draw elastic hysteresis curves.
7. Study two-dimensional collision of two balls.
8. Study a Fortin barometer and use it to measure atmospheric pressure.
9. Determine spring constant from a helical spring load-extension graph.
10. Study effect of surface nature on radiation emission and absorption.
11. Study energy conservation using a seconds pendulum.

### Practical subject matter
- Introduction: laboratory do’s/don’ts, analytical methods, basic equipment and procedures, handling reagent bottles, heating devices.
- Basic laboratory techniques: cutting and bending glass tube/rod, drawing a jet, boring a cork, heating solution in test tube/beaker/flask, filtration, measuring liquid volume, weighing, preparing standard oxalic acid solution.
- Purification/purity: crystallisation of an impure sample; melting point of a solid organic compound; boiling point of a liquid organic compound.
- Chemical equilibrium: equilibrium shift for ferric ions + thiocyanate ions; equilibrium shift between [Co(H2O)6]2+ and Cl−.
- pH: fruit juices; acid/base dilution; common-ion effect in weak acids/bases; pH of sodium chloride, ferric chloride and sodium carbonate solutions.
- Titrimetry (detailed text): endpoint and requirements; acidimetry/alkalimetry and indicators; strength of NaOH against standard oxalic acid; standard sodium carbonate solution; dilute HCl strength against standard sodium carbonate.
- Qualitative analysis: one cation and one anion in a supplied salt; systematic anion/cation analysis, dry and wet tests, and group analysis for Groups 0–VI.
- Organic analysis: detection of nitrogen, sulphur and halogens in an organic compound.

### Projects (8)
1. Investigate bacterial water contamination by checking sulphide-ion concentration and its cause.
2. Study water-purification methods.
3. Compare drinking-water hardness and iron/fluoride/chloride etc. across regions, including causes of values above permissible limits.
4. Compare foaming capacity of washing soaps and effect of sodium carbonate.
5. Compare acidity of tea-leaf samples and colour variation in prepared tea.
6. Study evaporation rates of different liquids.
7. Study effects of acids and alkalis on fibre tensile strength.
8. Study acids and mineral contents in vegetables and fruits.

The practical pages describe techniques and chemical systems, but do not provide a complete equipment quantity/size inventory. Any equipment inferred for these exercises must be tagged as an asset-design inference, not a board-specified item count.

## 4. Class XI — Biology (PDF pages 132–134)

- Botany major 4; Zoology major 4; Botany minor 3; Zoology minor 3; spotting 8; practical record 4; viva 4.

### Botany major (4 marks)
1. Study/describe locally available flowering plants from Fabaceae/Leguminosae, Solanaceae and Liliaceae families.
2. Prepare transverse-section slides and study dicot and monocot roots and stems.
3. Study plasmolysis in epidermal peels, e.g. Rhoeo leaves.
4. Study respiration rate in flower buds and germinating seeds.

### Zoology major (4 marks)
1. Study the effect of salivary amylase on starch.
2. Test urine for urea and bile salts.

### Botany minor (3 marks)
1. Distribution of stomata on upper/lower leaf surfaces.
2. Osmosis using a potato osmometer.
3. Separate plant pigments by paper chromatography.
4. Compare transpiration rates from upper/lower leaf surfaces.
5. Study imbibition in seeds/raisins.

### Zoology minor (3 marks)
1. Test suitable plant/animal materials for sugar, starch, protein and fats.
2. Test urine for sugar and albumin.

### Spotting (8 marks)
The list includes:
- Compound microscope parts.
- Specimens: bacteria, Oscillatoria, Spirogyra, Rhizopus, mushroom, yeast, liverwort, moss, fern, pine, monocot and dicot plants, lichen.
- Animal specimens: Amoeba, Hydra, liver fluke, Ascaris, leech, earthworm, prawn, silkworm, honeybee, snail, starfish, shark, Rohu, frog, lizard, pigeon and rabbit.
- Temporary/permanent slides showing plant and animal cells/tissues: palisade cells, guard cells, parenchyma, collenchyma, sclerenchyma, xylem, phloem, squamous epithelium, muscle fibres and mammalian blood smear.
- Mitosis slides of onion root tip or grasshopper somatic cells.
- Root, stem and leaf modifications; inflorescences; root/stem/leaf forms and venation.
- Experimental setups for anaerobic respiration, phototropism, apical bud removal and transpiration suction.
- Human skeleton and different joints.
- Frog external morphology using models/preserved specimens.

## 8. Important cross-document findings for LabVerse

1. **RBSE and NCERT must remain separate source packs.** RBSE provides the board curriculum and marks; NCERT manuals/kit specifications can inform scientific detail and apparatus specifications, but cannot silently replace or extend the RBSE list.
2. **Do not flatten choice structures.** “OR” branches in Chemistry and “any one/any three” in Biology need explicit choice groups in content schema.
3. **Mark totals and components need versioned rubrics.** Chemistry XI discrepancy and Physics XI time conflict must be stored as unresolved source issues pending teacher/board review.
4. **Board practical lists are not complete kit inventories.** Where apparatus is implied by a task but not named in the board source, mark it as inferred and seek the relevant NCERT kit manual/specification.
5. **No automatic unbounded chemistry sandbox.** Each reaction/qualitative test/functional group workflow must map to a reviewed, finite experiment rule and safe virtual representation.
6. **Biology slide/specimen content must preserve identity and observation limits.** Slide/model assets require authoritative identity, expected visible structures, labels and review.
7. **Research/project data require provenance.** Water-quality, soil, SPM, plant population, and comparative projects need dataset location/source, sampling design, units, uncertainty and permission/licence where data are external.
8. **Copyright:** both PDFs identify prescribed NCERT books/manuals as copyrighted. LabVerse should create original explanatory text and original 3D assets or use appropriately licensed material; do not copy board/NCERT artwork or manual pages into app assets without permission.
