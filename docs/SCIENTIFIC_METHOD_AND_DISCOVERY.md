# Scientific Method, Discovery and Integrity Policy

## LabVerse can assist research; it cannot bypass the scientific method

A software-generated explanation or simulation is not automatically a scientific discovery. The platform supports the workflow by making assumptions, evidence and tests explicit.

## Evidence levels

- A source claim is what a referenced source reports.
- An observation is what a defined measurement or viewing procedure records.
- A calculation is a result derived from declared inputs and method.
- A simulation is a result within a specified model and parameter range.
- A prediction is a candidate result that has not yet been observed.
- A hypothesis is a testable proposed explanation.
- A supported conclusion is a claim supported by the available evidence and its limitations.
- A discovery claim requires stronger independent evidence and review than an in-app simulation alone.

## Required scientific habits

1. State the question before selecting the desired answer.
2. Search for prior work and relevant counter-evidence.
3. Write testable hypotheses and alternative explanations.
4. Define independent, dependent and controlled variables.
5. Record sample selection, controls, repetitions and measurement limits.
6. Preserve raw data and disclose transformations.
7. Use valid calculations/statistics with correct units.
8. Distinguish association, prediction and causation.
9. Include negative, null and contradictory findings.
10. State uncertainty, assumptions, model validity range and limitations.
11. Reproduce results and seek independent review.
12. For empirical claims, identify what real-world validation is still needed.

## Novelty checking

A literature search can find relevant prior publications and related claims, but no finite search guarantees that an idea is globally novel. LabVerse may label an idea:
- NEW CANDIDATE — no close match found in the sources searched;
- RELATED PRIOR WORK FOUND;
- CONFLICTING EVIDENCE FOUND;
- NOVELTY NOT ASSESSED.

Never label an idea “first ever” or a proven discovery based only on keyword similarity or a generated summary.

## No fabrication

Do not fabricate papers, authors, DOIs, measurements, reactions, organisms, equations, references or data. An unavailable citation stays unavailable. Unsupported science is labelled NOT_MODELLED or NEEDS_REVIEW.

## Human and safety oversight

A researcher/teacher must review protocol safety and scientific claims. Hazardous chemical, biological or physical operations remain virtual and bounded. LabVerse does not provide an unrestricted recipe generator for unknown chemical mixing, pathogens, toxins, explosives or hazardous procedures. It does not replace institutional safety review, ethics review, clinical validation or regulatory approval.

## Public claims

Reports should distinguish “simulated in LabVerse”, “observed in a dataset”, “reported by a source”, “replicated in a real experiment” and “independently reviewed”. Never present a model output as a laboratory result that did not occur.


## Element and discovery-specific integrity rules

A chemical element is identified by proton number (atomic number Z). Ordinary chemical reactions can create compounds/materials but do not change the proton count of nuclei. A new-element claim belongs to nuclear science and requires experimental evidence and formal external evaluation, not an in-app mixing action or a visual animation.

For new-element work, LabVerse may teach nuclear identity, check sourced conservation balances, inspect permitted datasets, compare decay-event candidates and prepare an evidence dossier. It must keep model predictions, simulated events, imported detector data, reproduced evidence, independent review and formal IUPAC/IUPAP recognition distinct. A virtual event is never a measured detector event. A limited literature search cannot establish global novelty. The app must not mark any candidate as a formally recognised element.
