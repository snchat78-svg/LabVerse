## 2. Exercise 8 — blastula stage of mammalian embryonic development

Source: lelm206.pdf, Exercise 8.

- Required representation: permanent slide, chart, model or photograph of a transverse section of blastula; compound microscope and lens-cleaning material.
- Source-visible structures: blastocoel cavity, outer trophoblast layer and inner cell mass at one pole.
- Candidate IDs: BIO-XII-SLIDE-BLASTULA-TS, BIO-XII-MODEL-BLASTULA, BIO-XII-EMBRYO-MORULA-TO-BLASTULA.
- Model state should distinguish a solid morula from a cavity-containing blastula and label only source-supported structures.
- No embryo collection or handling. Use pre-prepared or synthetic virtual educational models. No diagnostic or reproductive-health interpretation.

## 3. Exercise 9 — Mendel's Law of Segregation

Source: lelm206.pdf, Exercise 9.

The source describes a bead simulation:
- 64 yellow and 64 green beads, same shape and size, represent contrasting alleles.
- F1 individuals are represented by one yellow and one green bead.
- F1 individuals are split into male/female groups; gametes are sampled and paired to generate F2.
- The procedure asks for six repeats and pooled genotype/phenotype data.
- The source explains an expected 3:1 dominant/recessive phenotype ratio and 1:2:1 genotype ratio.

Candidate IDs: BIO-XII-GENETICS-SEGREGATION-BEAD-SET, BIO-XII-GENETICS-F1-F2-PAIRING, BIO-XII-GENETICS-RATIO-ANALYSIS.

Scientific-model requirements:
- Make the sampling policy explicit and follow the source's documented procedure; do not hide whether sampling is with or without replacement.
- Use a reproducible random seed, but do not force a finite observed sample to exactly equal the theoretical 3:1 or 1:2:1 ratio.
- Store observed counts separately from expected probability ratios and explain finite-sample deviation.
- No prefilled learner observations presented as measured.

## 4. Exercise 10 — Law of Independent Assortment

Source: lelm207.pdf, Exercise 10.

The source uses four bead colours (64 each: yellow, green, red and white) to represent two traits, forms F1 bead clusters and randomly samples F2 combinations. It specifies six repeats, genotype/phenotype tabulation and an expected phenotypic 9:3:3:1 ratio.

Important limitation in the source: independent assortment is expected only for traits on different homologous chromosome pairs (not linked); linked genes are generally inherited together except where crossing-over separates them. The chapter mentions chi-square testing of deviations from expected ratios as later learning.

Candidate IDs: BIO-XII-GENETICS-INDEPENDENT-ASSORTMENT-BEADS, BIO-XII-GENETICS-DIHYBRID-CLUSTERS, BIO-XII-GENETICS-LINKAGE-CAVEAT.

Model requirements:
- Represent two traits and allele pairs explicitly.
- Expose the independent-assortment assumption; offer a separate linked-trait condition only after a reviewed model is created.
- Distinguish expected 9:3:3:1 probability from finite simulated counts.
- Retain random seed, repeats, pooled data, genotype counts, phenotype counts and assumptions.

## 5. Exercise 12 — controlled pollination by emasculation, bagging and tagging

Source: lelm209.pdf, Exercise 12.

The source requires large bisexual flowers, magnifying lens, tweezers, small sharp scissors, brush, alcohol, rubber bands, paper bags, clips and tags. It describes emasculation before anthesis, protecting the emasculated flower with a porous bag, transferring desired-parent pollen to the stigma, re-bagging and tagging the cross with parent identities/date.

Candidate IDs: BIO-XII-FLOWER-CONTROLLED-POLLINATION-MODEL, BIO-XII-FLOWER-EMASCULATION-STATE, BIO-XII-FLOWER-BAGGING-TAGGING, BIO-XII-POLLEN-TRANSFER-TO-STIGMA.

Workflow states should be reversible and stage-checked: bud-before-anthesis, emasculated, bagged, pollen transferred, re-bagged and tagged. The app must not claim fertilisation, seed set or offspring genotype without a separate validated biological outcome model. Keep scissors/tweezers as virtual tools; do not give unsupervised real-world cutting instructions.

## 6. Exercise 13 — staining nucleic acid by acetocarmine

Source: lelm209.pdf, Exercise 13.

The source lists onion epidermis, 2–4% acetocarmine/acetoorcein, slide/coverslip, brush/needle, scissors, filter paper and microscope, and describes stain and heat as part of the physical procedure.

Candidate IDs: BIO-XII-SLIDE-ONION-EPIDERMIS, BIO-XII-STAIN-ACETOCARMINE-VIRTUAL, BIO-XII-NUCLEUS-VISUALISATION.

Boundary: expose only pre-prepared/synthetic virtual slide and a visual stain-state model. Do not turn acetocarmine handling, hot spirit-lamp use, needle or cutting into home-lab instructions. Do not claim a stain result beyond the reviewed slide image/state.

## 7. Exercise 14 — disease-causing organisms and symptoms

Source: lelm210.pdf, Exercise 14.

The source requires preserved specimens, permanent slides or photographs for Ascaris, Entamoeba, Plasmodium, ringworm fungus and a compound microscope. It gives examples including Entamoeba histolytica, Plasmodium vivax, Ascaris lumbricoides and Trichophyton rubrum, with morphology and symptom descriptions.

Candidate IDs: BIO-XII-PATHOGEN-ENTAMOEBA-HISTOLYTICA, BIO-XII-PATHOGEN-PLASMODIUM-VIVAX, BIO-XII-PATHOGEN-ASCARIS-LUMBRICOIDES, BIO-XII-PATHOGEN-TRICHOPHYTON-RUBRUM.

Boundaries:
- Use approved prepared slides, source-linked images or original virtual models only; no culturing, handling, collection or infection experiments.
- This is morphology/educational identification, not clinical diagnosis or personalised medical advice.
- The chapter uses historical taxonomy labels such as Protozoa/Rhizopoda/Sporozoa/Aschelminthes/Deuteromycetes. Preserve these as source wording and map modern classification in a separate, versioned taxonomy record with specialist review.

## 8. Exercise 15 — soil texture

Source: lelm211.pdf, Exercise 15.

The source divides soil particles into sand (2–0.05 mm), silt (0.05–0.002 mm) and clay (less than 0.002 mm), describes a 12-class textural triangle and provides three methods. Method I specifies oven/stove-dried samples, a balance, weights, mechanical sieves and blotting paper; the shown mesh thresholds are 2 mm, 0.05 mm and 0.002 mm.

Candidate IDs: ENV-SOIL-SAMPLE, ENV-SOIL-SIEVE-2MM, ENV-SOIL-SIEVE-005MM, ENV-SOIL-SIEVE-0002MM, ENV-SOIL-TEXTURE-TRIANGLE.

Model requirements:
- Input fractions and sample mass; verify mass balance before calculating percentages.
- Use the textural triangle to classify fractions; show units and source class boundaries.
- Track source sample/site, moisture state, method and uncertainty.
- No fabricated soil data. If the learner uses a virtual sample, label all readings SIMULATED.

## 9. Exercise 16 — water-holding capacity of soil

Source: lelm212.pdf, Exercise 16.

The source defines water-holding capacity as water retained in capillary spaces after gravitational water percolates. It lists site-specific soil samples, a Gooch crucible with perforated base, filter paper, pestle/mortar, petri dish, beaker, glass rods, balance and blotting paper.

The observation calculation uses:
- A = crucible + blotting paper;
- B = crucible + blotting paper + dry soil before;
- C = B − A (dry soil);
- D = crucible + blotting paper + wet soil after;
- E = D − A (wet soil mass);
- N = E − C (water absorbed);
- percentage water-holding capacity = N/C × 100.

The source PDF screenshot visually shows 108°C for the oven option; parsed text can render this as “1080C”. This is a text-extraction artefact, not 1080°C.

Candidate IDs: ENV-SOIL-WHC-GOOCH-CRUCIBLE, ENV-SOIL-WHC-SAMPLE, ENV-SOIL-WHC-MASS-BALANCE.

Do not auto-populate values. A calculation validator must check all masses/units and reject negative or inconsistent water mass.

## 11. Exercise 21 — organisms in water samples

Source: lelm213.pdf, Exercise 21.

The source studies living organisms in water samples from different water bodies. It lists beakers, vials/test tubes, slides/coverslips, watch glasses, dropper, microscope and 5% FAA preservative; the procedure describes water sampling, fixation/preservation, sediment concentration and slide observation. Its annexure lists aquatic micro-organisms to support identification.

Candidate IDs: BIO-XII-WATER-SAMPLE, BIO-XII-WATER-MICROORGANISM-SLIDE, BIO-XII-AQUATIC-ORGANISM-CATALOGUE.

Safety: 5% FAA contains formalin, acetic acid and ethanol. It remains VIRTUAL_ONLY; do not instruct learners to prepare or use the fixative or to collect/preserve biological samples. Use pre-prepared slides, synthetic datasets or licensed images. Organism counts are observations only if a real sourced image/field dataset is used and its sampling method is recorded.

## 12. Exercises 22–24 — SPM, quadrat density and frequency

Source: lelm214.pdf, Exercises 22–24.

### Exercise 22 — suspended particulate matter
- Source method uses broad leaves at three sites representing high, medium and low traffic; five leaves per bundle; leaf area calculated by graph paper; bundle mass measured before and after; exposed for about two hours; calculate deposited particulate mass per leaf area.
- Candidate IDs: ENV-SPM-LEAF-COLLECTOR, ENV-SPM-SITE-OBSERVATION, ENV-SPM-MASS-AREA-ANALYSIS.
- LabVerse can run a simulated or imported-data workflow. Keep site coordinates, sample date/time, weather/context, mass, leaf surface area, blank/control and uncertainty. Do not invent measurements or present manual examples as live city data.

### Exercise 23 — plant population density by quadrat
- Source uses a 1 m × 1 m quadrat, five metres of cotton/nylon thread, four nails and hammer; ten random quadrats are sampled.
- Density formula in source: total individuals of a species across the sampling quadrats divided by the number of quadrats studied, with units expressed per unit area.
- Candidate IDs: BIO-XII-QUADRAT-1M, BIO-XII-SPECIES-COUNT, BIO-XII-POPULATION-DENSITY.

### Exercise 24 — plant population frequency
- Uses the same quadrat sampling setup; record presence/absence per species across ten random quadrats.
- Frequency = number of quadrats containing the species / total quadrats × 100.
- Candidate IDs: BIO-XII-POPULATION-FREQUENCY, BIO-XII-OCCURRENCE-MATRIX.
- Density and frequency must remain different calculations and result fields; do not collapse both into one “quadrat score”.

For both exercises, use anonymised species labels if identity is unknown; map taxon IDs only after authoritative identification. Do not fabricate field counts.

## 13. Exercise 25 — homologous and analogous organs

Source: lelm215.pdf, Exercise 25.

The source compares plant structures (tendrils/thorns, scale leaves/spines, stem/leaf tendrils, modified stems/roots, phylloclade/cladode/leaves) and animal structures (vertebrate forelimbs, insect/bird wings, mandibles). Requirements include plant specimens, potato/onion bulb/prickly pear/phylloclade/cladode and bird/cockroach/bat wings plus vertebral specimens.

Candidate IDs: BIO-XII-COMPARISON-HOMOLOGY-PLANT, BIO-XII-COMPARISON-ANALOGY-PLANT, BIO-XII-COMPARISON-HOMOLOGY-ANIMAL, BIO-XII-COMPARISON-ANALOGY-ANIMAL.

The teaching model must compare evolutionary/developmental origin separately from current function/appearance. Do not infer homology solely from superficial similarity. Species identity and examples require Biology-teacher review.
