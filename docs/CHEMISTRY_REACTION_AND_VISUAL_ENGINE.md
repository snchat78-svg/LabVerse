# Chemistry Reaction and Visual Engine

## Intended experience

Students place beakers, conical flasks, test tubes, burettes, pipettes, droppers, reagent bottles, balances and other syllabus-mapped equipment on the 3D bench. In a supported practical they select the correct sample/reagent, enter or adjust a quantity, perform an action and observe a live visual response.

## Chemistry state

A chemical sample is structured, not a free-text label. It may contain substance/ion/mixture IDs; amount, concentration and units; solvent and volume; temperature; pH/acidity model where available; phase and sample container; reaction progress; and experiment-specific conditions/limitations.

## Rule-driven outcomes

A reviewed rule may calculate or select: whether reaction is expected under stated conditions; balanced equation and known products; limiting reagent/stoichiometry where in scope; colour or indicator endpoint; precipitate and appearance; gas evolution and safe visual indicator; pH/indicator transition; temperature trend or energy indication if sourced; characteristic flame-test colour for a validated syllabus activity; observation and explanation.

Each rule includes valid conditions, evidence/source reference, expected observations, readiness, limitations and tests.

## Example: virtual flame-test practical

The student selects the practical, places required apparatus and sample, then performs an allowed simulated action. An animated virtual flame appears. Its characteristic colour is selected from the approved ion/result rule and displayed in the observation panel. Explanation links the selected sample/ion to the characteristic emission-colour observation.

The colour is not a generic decoration. It is linked to the selected sample ID and validated rule. Unknown ion/sample means the interface says no validated colour rule for this sample; it must not invent a colour. A flame animation is an educational representation, not a temperature or spectrum measurement.

## Example: mixing two solutions

1. Validate selected solutions, containers and volumes.
2. Find an approved reaction rule for that pair and conditions.
3. Compute outcome or explicit no-reaction/not-modelled state.
4. Emit colour, precipitate, gas bubbles, pH or temperature observations only if the rule defines them.
5. Animate the corresponding sample state.
6. Show balanced equation, observation, inference and reasoning separately.
7. Record quantities, conditions, result and source.

## Visual channels

- Solution colour: material colour/opacity transition from rule-defined observation.
- Precipitate: particles/clouding/sediment with declared appearance and timing.
- Gas: bubbles and gas-state indicator; bubbles alone do not identify a gas.
- Indicator: calibrated pH range or experiment-specific endpoint logic.
- Flame: animated visual profile keyed to approved observation.
- Heat: thermometer/energy indicator only where supported by the experiment model.

## Scientific boundaries

First release is a curated syllabus-bounded reaction set, not arbitrary chemistry prediction. Do not extrapolate a product, colour, gas or temperature to unverified conditions. Hazardous substances and operations are supervised virtual scenarios. No real ignition or chemical-dispensing hardware integration is part of the first release.

## Required tests

For each rule: valid reactants, wrong reactant, missing conditions, quantity boundary, expected observation, no-reaction where applicable, unsupported sample, reset/replay and visual-to-observation consistency.
